Cybersecurity: The Basics

October 5, 2025 | Michele Siddi

Cybersecurity is the set of techniques and processes to protect systems, networks and data from cyber attacks, theft and damage. In an increasingly connected world, cybersecurity has become an absolute priority for businesses and individuals.

🔐 Key Concepts: The CIA Triad

Information security is based on three fundamental pillars, known as the CIA Triad:

  • Confidentiality: Ensuring that only authorized individuals can access sensitive information. This includes the use of encryption, multi-factor authentication and strict access controls.
  • Integrity: Protecting data from unauthorized or accidental modifications. Data must remain accurate and reliable throughout its lifecycle.
  • Availability: Ensuring that services and data are always accessible when needed to authorized users, protecting systems from disruptions and attacks that could make them unusable.

⚠️ Common Threats in 2025

The cyber threat landscape is constantly evolving. Here are the most common ones:

  • Malware: Malicious software including viruses, trojans, ransomware and spyware. Ransomware, in particular, encrypts data and demands a ransom for their recovery.
  • Phishing and Social Engineering: Deception techniques via email, SMS or calls to obtain sensitive information or system access.
  • DDoS Attacks: Intentional overload of systems to make them inaccessible to legitimate users.
  • Credential Theft: Passwords and sensitive data stolen through data breaches, keyloggers or brute force attacks.
  • Zero-Day Vulnerabilities: Unknown security flaws that are exploited before a patch is released.

🛡️ How to Protect Yourself: Best Practices

Implementing good security practices is essential to reduce risks:

  • Strong and unique passwords: Use complex passwords (at least 12 characters, with uppercase, lowercase, numbers and symbols) and different ones for each account. Consider using a password manager.
  • Two-factor authentication (2FA): Add an extra layer of security by requiring a second verification method beyond the password.
  • Regular updates: Always keep software, operating systems and applications updated to fix security vulnerabilities.
  • Regular backups: Make frequent backups of important data and store them in secure and separate locations (3-2-1 rule: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite).
  • Staff training: Most attacks succeed due to human error. Train the team on security and current threats.
  • Firewall and Antivirus: Use modern security solutions and keep them updated.
  • Principle of least privilege: Grant users only the permissions strictly necessary to do their job.

💼 Cybersecurity for Businesses

Organizations must adopt a structured approach to security:

  • Conduct regular security assessments to identify vulnerabilities
  • Implement an incident response plan to manage any breaches
  • Comply with applicable regulations (GDPR, NIS2, etc.)
  • Consider adopting security frameworks such as ISO 27001 or NIST
  • Invest in monitoring and detection solutions to identify anomalies in real-time

Conclusion: Investing in cybersecurity is no longer optional: it is fundamental for the protection and growth of every digital reality. Security is an ongoing process that requires vigilance, constant updating and a culture of awareness at all levels of the organization.
